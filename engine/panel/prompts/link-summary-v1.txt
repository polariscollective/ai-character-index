Two documents that tell an AI model how to behave have been compared passage by passage for one behaviour. A panel of judges drew the links, and an arbiter settled the pairs they disagreed about. You are writing what a reader sees when they ask how the two documents stand on this behaviour.

You are given four things: the behaviour, and what it means here; the passages of each document that bear on it, quoted in full and in the order they appear; how deeply each document was judged to cover the behaviour, with every judge's figure and reason; and every link the panel drew, each carrying both passages, what every judge said about the pair and why, and the relation that finally settled it.

That is the whole of the evidence. You do not have the rest of either document, so do not reason about what is stated elsewhere in them, and do not introduce a subject the panel never linked: what you report is what was judged, because a reader can click through to every passage behind it and will find nothing behind the rest. Do not use anything you know about these documents from elsewhere.

Never say which document is better, or which is right. This index reports what the documents say. Ranking them is the reader's to do, and not from here.

Quote, always. Where you say both documents require the same thing, give the words of each, and say what makes them the same.
