You are writing one short paragraph saying why a specification was judged to cover a behaviour as deeply as it was.

You are given the behaviour, the specification, the figure the panel arrived at, and what each judge wrote when they gave their own score. Those reasons are your whole material.

WHAT TO WRITE

One paragraph. What it is in the document that earns this figure: what the document does about this behaviour, in what form, and what it stops short of. The scale runs from a behaviour merely named, through discussed in general terms, to concrete rules a grader could quote, to rules with worked examples, so what usually decides a figure is which of those the document reaches.

Say where the panel did not agree. If one judge scored above or below the others, say that the panel divided and on what, in a clause. A mean that hides a disagreement is a figure a reader would be wrong to trust.

NEVER NAME A JUDGE

Not by name, not by model, not by number, not as "one judge", "the first" or "two of them". Write about the panel, or about the reading itself: "the panel divided over whether the examples amount to an answer key" rather than anything that identifies who held which view. A reader of this index has no way to know what those names mean and no reason to weigh one above another.

Do not write the figure itself. It sits directly above this paragraph, and repeating it spends the reader's attention on something they have already read.

HOW TO WRITE IT

Name the specification rather than calling it "this document" or "the spec".

Quote the document where a judge quoted it. Everything else is the reading, and should read as one.

Do not rank, and do not praise or fault. A document that stops at general terms is described as stopping there, and that is the whole of it. This index reports what specifications say; whether that is enough is not a question it answers.

No em-dashes. No passage numbers, locators, section anchors or version strings.

LENGTH

At most 90 words. This sits under a figure in a grid, above a longer passage about how the specification compares with the others, and a reader who has to work through it has been handed a third thing to read rather than an explanation of the first.

Answer with the paragraph and nothing else.
