    How each lab governs the rules its models follow

    Some AI companies publish a rulebook for how their models should behave, usually called a model specification. This view scores how that rulebook is run: whether it exists, what it covers, whether a change to it can be seen, and whether the public gets a say before it is weakened. Six companies, as of September 2026; the scores are Polaris Collective's own and open to challenge.

    How to read this ranking

    Having a rulebook and covering something are scored apart. Alibaba shows why. On a single "has a rulebook" column, a rulebook that names no model would score the same as one that binds a company's products, and the two are not the same thing.

    Read the checks, not only the total. The totals put OpenAI and Anthropic one point apart, but they fall short in opposite ways. OpenAI keeps a record of its versions and does not list its firm limits; Anthropic lists its firm limits and keeps no record of its versions. A single number hides the most useful thing in the comparison.

    The bottom of the ranking comes down to the first question. Three of the six companies publish no rulebook, so most of the grid cannot apply to them, and they sit at the bottom for that one reason. To compare the companies that do publish, look at the change record. To see the case for publishing at all, look at the totals.

    A caveat. The four questions were written to argue for change, not to measure. Turning them into a scored grid gives them a precision they were not designed for, and the descriptions of each score are ours. Anyone could move a company by a few points by reading a description differently. What does not move is the overall picture: every company falls short on the change record, none offers a comment period before loosening a firm limit, and none covers government, defence and other special deployments.

    How we scored

    The four questions are the four asks of a transparency proposal Polaris Collective has been drafting since 10 September 2026. Each is split into checks scored from 0 to 4. The first two questions have three checks each, because together they are the minimum the proposal asks for; the other two have two. The total is out of 40.

    The tables below say what earns 0, 2 and 4 on each check. A score of 1 or 3 falls between the descriptions either side of it.

    Supporting practices

    Five further practices, taken from Kembery and colleagues, Emerging International Best Practices for AI Model Specs, keeping only those an outsider can check from public sources. Each is scored 0, 1 or 2, for a total out of 10. They are reported beside the ranking and not counted in it, because they measure neighbouring good practice rather than how changes are governed.

      What we could not check

      Researched on 18 September 2026 from primary sources, in six parallel searches for evidence, one per company. Every score rests on a document we found and dated. These points are not yet confirmed.

      • Alibaba's rulebook was not read at its own address. The page at s.alibaba.com/aaig/specification is built in the browser, and our tools could not retrieve it. The text we used comes from this index, which holds 210 passages of the document, word for word, at its April 2026 version. The index keeps only passages that bear on a behaviour, so it skips prefaces and boilerplate. Two things reported elsewhere as part of the preface, an invitation to public comment and an acknowledgement of safeguards applied by the service around the model, are neither confirmed nor ruled out. A statement of coverage in the preface could change Alibaba's score on check 1.2. The page has yet to be read in a browser.
      • Archived copies could not be checked. The Internet Archive (web.archive.org) could not be reached throughout, so we could not detect silent edits to any undated page. This matters most for Google, where three of the relevant documents carry no date at all.
      • Whether Anthropic has signed the EU Code of Practice is unconfirmed. Its July 2025 post says it "intends to sign"; its own page of voluntary commitments does not list it. No score depends on this.
      • Some details of Claude Mythos rest partly on secondary sources, though the absence of a published rulebook for it is confirmed from Anthropic's own pages.
      • Meta's log of updates to its Community Standards could not be read, because it too is built in the browser, so our view that it does not cover AI is an inference rather than a check.
      • All scores are ours. None comes from an established, published scoring method.
